What is a Stock?

The Basics

The Core Idea

A stock is a tiny ownership slice of a corporation. When you purchase stock, you officially become a shareholder[cite: 1].

Real-World Example

Imagine a pizza shop sliced into 100 equal shares. Buying just 1 share means you officially own 1% of that business[cite: 1]!

Why Do Prices Change?

Market Dynamics

Supply & Demand

Prices fluctuate dynamically based on buyer and seller volume. High buyer interest pushes prices up; high seller volume pushes prices down[cite: 1].

Example Impact

A gaming studio launches a worldwide blockbuster hit. Massive demand drives the price upward instantly:

$10 → $14[cite: 1]

How Do People Make Money?

Returns & Profits

Capital Appreciation

Purchase shares, hold them through growth, and sell them later at a higher market valuation[cite: 1].

Dividends

Successful, profitable corporations regularly distribute a portion of their net profits directly to shareholders as cash dividends[cite: 1].

What is a Mutual Fund?

Diversified Growth

The Pool Concept

A mutual fund pools money from thousands of individual investors to buy a massive basket of varied stocks and bonds automatically.

Why Choose Mutual Funds?

Instead of manually researching individual companies, professional fund managers handle the diversification for you, significantly lowering overall investment risk.
