What is a Stock?

The Basics

The Core Idea

A stock represents fractional ownership in a corporation. When you purchase stock, you officially become a shareholder[cite: 1].

Real-World Example

Imagine a pizza shop split into 100 equal shares. Buying just 1 share means you own 1% of that business[cite: 1]!

What is an IPO?

Going Public

Initial Public Offering

An IPO is when a private company sells stock to the general public for the very first time to raise expansion capital.

From Private to Public

Before an IPO, only founders and early private investors own shares. Post-IPO, anyone can buy shares on major exchanges like NASDAQ or NYSE.

IPO Guide Slide 3 of 11

How to List on a Stock Market

Listing Process

1. Preparation & Auditing

Companies must meet strict financial reporting regulations, file official paperwork with regulators (like the SEC), and hire investment banks.

2. Valuation & Exchange

The company sets an initial share price, lists on an exchange (e.g., NASDAQ), and opens trading bells so public investors can place buy orders.

Why Do Prices Change?

Supply & Demand

Buyer & Seller Pressure

Prices fluctuate dynamically based on supply and demand. More buyers push prices up; more sellers push prices down[cite: 1].

Example Impact

A gaming studio makes a blockbuster hit. High public demand drives share pricing higher:

$10 → $14[cite: 1]

What is a Mutual Fund?

Diversification

The Pooled Fund Concept

A mutual fund pools money from thousands of investors to buy a massive diversified basket of stocks, bonds, and assets automatically.

Professional Management

Expert fund managers oversee portfolio choices for you, reducing individual risk compared to buying single standalone stocks.
